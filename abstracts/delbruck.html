Neuromorphic silicon retina event cameras

Tobi Delbrück (Sensors Group, Institute of Neuroinformatics, University of Zurich and ETH Zurich)
Friday, October 16, 2026, 10:30 am · Dilworth Room, Simons Hall, Institute for Advanced Study

Brain spiking activity is only about 0.1% active based on 1 Hz average spike rate and 1 kHz internal neuron bandwidth. This observation suggests that sparse, activity-driven long range communication is a key to power efficiency of brains. Neuromorphic event camera silicon retinas are a clear example of exploiting this basic principle. Their quick (low latency) sparse output enables quickly-reactive robots that burn power only on demand and work in challenging lighting conditions. I will demonstrate our DAVIS camera that outputs brightness change events, monochrome frames, and inertial measurements, and show some applications of event cameras in surveillance and robotics. The talk will also briefly describe our most recent SciDVS camera that targets wide-field neuroimaging, and an example of a state of art industrial hybrid frame-event sensor. Time permitting, the talk will introduce a project started at IAS to use a pair of early DVS128 event cameras with fisheye lenses for fly-inspired visual-inertial navigation.
