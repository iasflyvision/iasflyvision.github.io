Optimization principles and the fly’s computation of visual motion

William Bialek (Princeton University)
Friday, November 20, 2026, 10:30 am · Dilworth Room, Simons Hall, Institute for Advanced Study

On a dark night our visual system can count single photons. Many insects have compound eyes that reach the limits of resolution set by diffraction. These and other examples have led many people to explore whether optimization principles can provide a more general guide to thinking about neural computation. This goes under many names—optimality, normative theories, perception as Bayesian inference, …. At the core, these approaches predict that the structure of computation should be matched to the statistics of the inputs. If we know these statistics then we have an essentially parameter free theory of what the brain should compute. But we usually have only parametric guesses at the relevant distributions. I’ll describe recent progress on sampling the joint distribution of movies and motion that is relevant for flies, and what we are learning about the rich structure of the optimal estimator.
