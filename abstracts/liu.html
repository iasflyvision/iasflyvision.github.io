Artificial insect-inspired neuromorphic motion systems: Learning the tradeoffs of analog full-custom integrated circuit silicon motion models

Shih-Chii Liu (Sensors Group, Institute of Neuroinformatics, University of Zurich and ETH Zurich)
Friday, October 2, 2026, 9:00 am · Bloomberg Hall, Institute for Advanced Study

In this talk, I present the components of a 1-dimensional full custom silicon fly-inspired direction-selective chip designed in late 1990s (Liu, 2000, 2001). This chip includes the adaptation properties of the retina, the bandpass filtering of the lamina, and the normalizing integrating properties of the wide-field direction selective cell. I will also describe the challenges faced when designing these analog modules and how we obtain a functional system by introducing ON-ON and OFF-OFF pathways for the correlator/multiplier blocks. While micro aerial vehicles can benefit from these fast, compact, low power sensors, I will discuss some of the challenges from the inflexibility of fully-integrated sensors and how we moved to the design of a motion sensor which combines a 20x20 pixel silicon retina front-end sensor and a dsPIC microcontroller optimized for digital signal processing. The motion system can be used for evaluation of algorithms on the flying platform. In particular, we developed a 2D implementation of the image interpolation algorithm (I2A) for global optical flow. We will describe this sensor platform, the I2A algorithm, and measurements obtained from it. This sensor platform (Liu et al, 2015), weighs 6g (without lens), burns 170mW, and computes optical flow at up to 1kHz sample rate with 12% occupancy of the DSP cycles.
