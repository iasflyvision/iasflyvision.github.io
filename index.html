Description

A unique opportunity to experience the intellectual environment of the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) in Princeton, the storied home of Einstein, von Neumann, and Oppenheimer. The seminar will serve as an on-ramp to research in computational neuroscience, focusing on the field’s ongoing transformation by connectomics:

  • Brain emulation. Neuronal wiring diagrams, or connectomes, now make it possible to simulate a brain with unprecedented realism.
  • Graph algorithms. The recent Drosophila connectome is inspiring research on algorithms for ordering, matching, and generation of weighted directed graphs.
  • Normative theories. Connectomics is providing insights into what and how the brain computes, stimulating new normative theories of why the brain computes.
  • Neuromorphic VLSI. By revealing the physical layout of brain wiring, connectomics can inform the design of integrated circuits that replicate brain functions.

Students will spend every Friday morning at the IAS, learning through formal and informal interactions with leading researchers in residence at the 2026–27 Special Year Program on Modeling Fly Vision. Students will also receive practical guidance on choosing a research project, writing a project proposal, designing experiments, evaluating progress, and managing project scope.

The final deliverable will be a research project proposal including preliminary results, similar to a senior thesis or Ph.D. thesis proposal.

Requirements

  • Three hours in class and research seminar per week (9 am–12 noon on Friday)
  • Research project proposal

Prerequisites

  • Expertise in linear algebra, and a programming language like Python.
  • Familiarity with neural nets, e.g., COS 324, 485, 486.

Recommended

  • Basic knowledge about graph theory and algorithms, information theory, visual perception, computer vision, electronic circuits, and/or biological neurons will be helpful.
  • Exposure to continuous mathematics, and basics of ordinary differential equations.

Assessments

  • In-class participation 30%
  • Oral presentations 35%
  • Final project paper 35%
